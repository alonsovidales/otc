Off The Cloud

Privacy

What the Off The Cloud bridge keeps about you, why, and for how long. Last updated 3 October 2026.

Your photos, videos and files are on your own device, not here. Off The Cloud is a device in your home. This page is about the bridge at off-the.cloud: the service that gives your device a name, lets you reach it from outside your home, and connects it with your friends' devices. You only need it, and an account, for that. A device used at home (at http://otc.local:8080) or through Tailscale Funnel never talks to us.

The bridge relays your connection; it does not store what passes through it. Read how the relay works: it is not end-to-end encrypted.

Who we are

The bridge is run by Off The Cloud, the controller for the data on this page. Write to info@off-the.cloud about anything here, or use the contact form.

What is stored on your device is yours: you decide what goes on it and who sees it, and we have no access to it.

How the relay works

Devices sit behind home routers, so the apps and browsers connect to the bridge and the bridge passes the connection to your device. The connection to the bridge is encrypted (HTTPS/TLS), and so is the device's own connection to it, but the bridge itself decrypts and re-encrypts the traffic: it is not end-to-end encrypted. While a request passes through, its content is in the bridge's memory. It is not stored or logged, and only the traffic counts above are kept.

Your device password never crosses the bridge in readable form: the apps encrypt it with a key only your device holds. Everything on your device is encrypted at rest with a key derived from that password.

If you would rather nothing passed through us, use your device at home, through Tailscale Funnel, or with a bridge of your own; the bridge is open source too.

Who else is involved

  • OVH (France) hosts the bridge's servers and its domain name, in the EU.
  • Apple and Google: if you sign in with them, they confirm who you are and give us your email and name. Push notifications to iPhones go through Apple, and to Android phones through Google (Firebase Cloud Messaging). Both are in the US and certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.
  • Proton (Switzerland, which the EU recognises as protecting data adequately) sends our emails - the link to confirm your email and password-reset links - and receives what you write to info@off-the.cloud.
  • GitHub (US, Data Privacy Framework): devices download their software updates from GitHub directly, which sees your device's internet address.

No one else receives your data, unless the law requires it.

Your rights

You can see, correct and download everything the bridge keeps about you, and delete it, from your account page: Download my data gives it to you as a file, and Delete my account removes it. Your devices then go on working at home. You can also object to what we keep for our legitimate interests, ask us to limit it, or ask anything at info@off-the.cloud; we answer within a month.

You can complain to a data protection authority. In the Netherlands it is the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens; you can also go to the one where you live.

The apps

The Off The Cloud apps talk to your device, through the bridge when you are away. The iPhone and Android apps send your photos and videos to your device and keep your device password in the phone's secure storage. The map in a photo's details comes from OpenStreetMap on Android and Apple Maps on iPhone, which receive the map area being shown. The apps contain no analytics or advertising.

Age

Accounts are for people aged 16 and over.

Changes

If this changes in a way that matters, we tell account holders by email before it applies.
