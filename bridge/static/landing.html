Your photos, your rules.
Off the cloud, for good.

Off The Cloud is a NAS and a social network in one small box you own outright: your photos, videos, and documents back up in full resolution, get AI-tagged for instant search, and stay behind RAID-1 protected disks — with an optional, ethical social feed for sharing memories with the people who actually matter, free of ads, algorithms, and doomscrolling.

Get your device running

One command on any machine you can already log into: Ubuntu, Debian, Raspberry Pi OS and other Debian-based distros (64-bit).

Terminal 
# log into the machine, then:
curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/alonsovidales/otc/main/scripts/install.sh \
  | sudo bash -s -- <subdomain>

It installs everything and builds the software from source, then starts the service at http://<device-ip>:8080/. Safe to re-run to update. RAID-1 storage and the WiFi setup are not part of this path — see the image for a from-scratch Raspberry Pi.

For a Raspberry Pi 5 with at least 8 GB of RAM, a MicroSD card for the system and, optionally, two USB disks for RAID-1. No keyboard, monitor or Linux knowledge needed.
⬇ Download the image (≈ 530 MB)
  1. Flash it to the MicroSD card with Raspberry Pi Imager: "Use custom", pick the file, write. Skip Imager's own customisation step.
  2. Plug in the disks, insert the card and power the Pi on. After a minute it opens a WiFi network called Off The Cloud.
  3. Join it from your phone: the setup page opens by itself. It asks for your WiFi, a name for the device and how to use the disks, then installs the software - about 20 minutes.
  4. Open https://<name>.off-the.cloud when it's done and choose your password.

The image only carries the setup wizard: it installs the current release, so it never goes out of date. Checksum: .sha256.

Get started

What happens under the hood

Both paths end in the same place: the image just runs the install script for you, after asking for your WiFi, a name and your disks from a phone.

1
Run the script

It installs MariaDB, Go, ONNX Runtime and the tagging model, then builds and starts the otc service, reachable straight away at http://<device-ip>:8080/ on your local network — safe to re-run any time to pick up updates.

2
Pick a subdomain

This just names the device for later, e.g. pit for pit.off-the.cloud — it only does something once you have Bridge access: that's the address your apps and friends would then use to reach the device from outside your home network, instead of the local :8080 address.

3
Pair an app

Open the web, iOS, Android, macOS or Windows client, point it at your subdomain, and start backing up.

Want RAID-1 out of the box? Use the Raspberry Pi 5 image: it wipes and mirrors two USB disks during setup, and if a Pi ever dies you flash a new card, attach the same disks and the wizard offers to recover everything. The status LEDs and every configuration option are in the README.
What it is

One device, four ways to reach it

A Raspberry Pi in your home stores everything; native apps on every platform keep it in sync and let you browse, search, and share it.

A Raspberry Pi 5 with two SD cards in RAID-1, storing your originals unprocessed and uncompressed — encrypted at rest, key never stored on the device itself.

iOS and Android apps that back up your camera roll in the background, in full resolution, and double as the social feed.

FAQ

Questions people actually ask

Do I need to buy special hardware?

A Raspberry Pi 5 (8GB) with two micro-SD cards for RAID-1 storage, a power supply, and an active cooler — all off-the-shelf, roughly €250 all in for 1TB of protected storage. See the README for the exact recommended parts list.

What is "the Bridge", and do I need it?

Your device isn't directly reachable from the internet by default. The Bridge is an optional relay service that lets your own apps and friends reach your device from outside your home network — without it, OTC still works fully at home, you'd just need your own port-forwarding/static IP setup to reach it remotely. Use the contact form below to request Bridge access.

Is my data actually private?

Files are encrypted at rest, and the encryption key is never stored on the device. Nothing is sold, used to train AI models, or shown to anyone you haven't explicitly approved as a friend. AI photo tagging also runs locally on the device — your photos never leave it to be tagged.

Is it really open source?

Yes — the device, bridge, and every client are all open source on GitHub. You can audit it, self-host the bridge yourself, or build your own device image from source.

What if I don't want the social network part?

It's entirely optional. Use OTC purely as a NAS for backup and file sync if that's all you want — the social feed only shows content from friends you've explicitly accepted.

Is there a pre-built image I can just flash?

Yes — for a Raspberry Pi 5 with at least 8 GB of RAM: see the Raspberry Pi 5 image tab at the top. Flash it, power the Pi on, join its "Off The Cloud" WiFi from your phone and follow the setup page. For any other Debian/Ubuntu machine, use the install script.
